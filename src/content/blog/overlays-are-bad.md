Several companies have bought into the idea that accessibility [widgets such as overlays](https://overlayfactsheet.com/#what-is-a-web-accessibility-overlay) are their saving grace when in fact, [overlays do more harm than good](https://youtu.be/R12Z1Sp-u4U). In addition to this, the [EU and IAAP have released a joint statement](https://www.edf-feph.org/accessibility-overlays-dont-guarantee-compliance-with-european-legislation/) pointing out that overlays don't guarantee compliance. According to [Usablenet's year-end report](https://info.usablenet.com/2022-year-end-digital-accessibility-lawsuit-report-download-page), companies using widgets ( a.k.a overlays ) received 575 lawsuits which is a 36% jump over last year. If your company uses an overlay to "fix" accessibility issues with your product, I invite you to read [https://overlayfactsheet.com](https://overlayfactsheet.com) and reconsider. Haben Girma, a Deaf Blind lawyer, released a video asking companies to avoid them as well.

%[https://youtu.be/R12Z1Sp-u4U]

The best solution is to perform an accessibility audit and update any accessibility issues found. Speaking as a Front-end Engineer and certified accessibility professional, I understand the challenges teams face when updating legacy code. I've been there. In fact, that space is where I spend most of my time. So in that same light, as an engineer, I know it's not impossible. It takes time, but it can be done. All we have to do is right bad code.
