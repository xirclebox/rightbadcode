Accessibility is a part of UX, and If you're not including accessibility reviews in your UX process, you're already behind the ball. The reason is you have either consciously or subconsciously chosen to limit your design decisions and research to abled individuals who use your product in a similar manner as you. This sounds like a pretty bold statement to make, but I'm speaking from experience, both as a teammate and as someone who made this very mistake in the past. I've been in the room behind the glass or watching pre-recorded sessions observing a group of people use a website. One thing I can say for certain is they all used a mouse. While my team and I may have come away with great insight into how people navigate our site, we only saw one perspective — someone who can use a mouse just like us. We had zero clue how someone with a motor disability would navigate our site. Even though the demographics of our personas varied in multiple ways, not one was a disabled user or involved the use of assistive tech of any kind.
